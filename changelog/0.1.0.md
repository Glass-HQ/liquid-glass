date: 2026-11-16
title: Specular highlights, a color picker, and menus without morphing
---

### New

- `GlassRow`: a row in a glass panel, as a menu's items are. Where it nears the panel's corners it follows their curve moved inward, and its side padding keeps its content clear of the curve. Use it for a select's options or a command palette's results, so every list on glass has the same concentric rows. Glass menus now use it.
- `tint` takes any CSS color in the DOM scene, including a theme variable such as `var(--accent)` or an `oklch()` color. It resolves where the glass is and follows the variable as the theme changes.

- `specular` lights the rim of glass with two equal highlights on opposite corners, top left and bottom right at rest, as on iOS. They turn around the glass to face the pointer, and follow the device's tilt where the browser reports it without asking for permission (Android). It is off by default. Set it on a scene, or on any surface or control:

  ```tsx
  <GlassScene specular>
    …
  </GlassScene>

  <GlassButton specular>Get started</GlassButton>
  ```

  The highlight is drawn on a small layer inside each surface, so moving the light never redraws the content filter. Reduced motion keeps them at rest; reduced transparency and forced colors hide it.

- `GlassColorPicker` is a hue and saturation wheel. A small dot of the chosen color marks it exactly; pressing the wheel grows the dot into a Lens glass bubble that follows the pointer, with a point at its center on the exact color:

  ```tsx
  const [color, setColor] = useState("#0a84ff");

  <GlassColorPicker value={color} onValueChange={setColor} name="color" />;
  ```

  Arrow keys change hue and saturation. Set `--lg-backdrop` to the color behind the picker, so the lens refracts it at the wheel's edge.

- `--lg-backdrop` gives glass a body over transparent content. Glass takes its look from what is behind it, so a control in a small scene of its own, over nothing, showed only its rims while a menu over the app had a frosted body. Set the variable to the color behind the scene, on the scene or any ancestor, and Regular and Clear glass treat it as their backdrop wherever the content is transparent, looking as they would over content of that color:

  ```css
  .toolbar {
    --lg-backdrop: var(--window-background);
  }
  ```

  ```tsx
  <GlassScene className="toolbar">
    <GlassContent />
    <GlassTabs aria-label="View" items={items} />
  </GlassScene>
  ```

  The color paints only through the glass: the content and the space around the glass stay transparent, so a scene can still sit over a transparent window. A new color applies without rebuilding the filter. Unset, glass draws exactly as before.

- `GlassSwitch` and `GlassSlider` accept `disabled`. A disabled control is dimmed, ignores pointer, drag and keyboard input, and is announced as disabled; a disabled switch also leaves the tab order, and its form input is disabled:

  ```tsx
  <GlassSwitch disabled checked={sync} onCheckedChange={setSync} aria-label="Sync" />
  <GlassSlider disabled aria-label="Volume" defaultValue={50} />
  ```

### Improved

- Tabs move as macOS 27's do, measured on its tabs. The chosen tab rests on a small plate of glass; pressing lifts a magnifying Lens out of it that grows past the tab, and the plate fades beneath it. Dragging carries the lens with the pointer at the size it lifted at, instead of resizing it to every label it passes over, so it no longer jitters between widths; it squashes with speed, wobbles as it stops, and takes an end tab's size only at either end of the bar. Letting go settles it onto the tab under its centre at that tab's size. The bar no longer grows under a press unless `interactive` is set. The lifted lens bends in a thin band at its rim with colored fringes there, as macOS's pressed controls do, while what is under its middle stays sharp; pressed switch and slider thumbs use the same lens.

- Glass is much faster in Chromium and Electron. Each surface now draws its own glass as its own backdrop filter, instead of the scene filtering its whole content layer, so hovering a menu row, opening a popup or typing under glass redraws only the glass over it rather than the whole window. A filter carries only small tiles of each map: four corners and the single row and column between them. In Glass's desktop app, hovering an open menu went from about 8 to over 40 frames a second. In Chromium, surfaces get inline `backdrop-filter`, `border-radius` and `box-shadow` while they are added, and a surface with glass inside it draws its glass on its `::before`; see [Without React](../packages/liquid-glass/README.md#without-react).

- Glass changes with its size, as on macOS 27, where every value of the glass filter follows the shorter side of the shape. Measured on `NSGlassEffectView` at 31 sizes and checked against captures over black, gray, white, text and a photo:
  - Glass up to 64px (buttons, capsules, toolbar groups) stays nearly clear: text behind it remains readable, bright backdrops stay brighter in dark appearance, and the rim bends by half the height instead of a fixed 60px.
  - From 64px to 160px glass gathers its panel look: frost, a darker face in dark appearance and a wide, soft shadow.
  - Regular glass blends a softer copy of its backdrop into its face, keeping the darker of the two in dark appearance and the lighter in light, so fine bright detail melts on dark glass and fine dark detail on light glass while the rest stays sharp.
- The rim matches macOS's, measured on native glass over black, gray, white and a photo:
  - The key light from above and the fill light from below now add to the glass instead of painting white over it, so they read the same over light and dark backdrops (+62 levels on Regular, more on Clear over brighter backdrops). They fall off within about a pixel and a half and fade out around the shape by 70 degrees, rather than wrapping into the corners.
  - The outline darkens the backdrop's outermost pixel all the way around the shape, by an amount that depends on what is behind it, as macOS does, instead of a black line on the sides only.
  - Regular glass shades its face faintly along a ring a few pixels inside its top and side edges.
  - Small glass keeps more of the color behind it: less saturation on controls and on Clear glass.
  - Material maps now have three planes (field, rim and light) instead of two; `MaterialMaps.overlay` is replaced by `rim` and `light`.
- Menus open and close cleanly. Submenus open the moment the pointer reaches their item (`GlassMenuSubmenuTrigger` `delay` now defaults to 0). A popup closing stays on screen while the popup opening in its place waits for its glass, so moving between submenus never leaves a gap; a new popup shows its contents only once its glass is drawn. Menus are placed in viewport coordinates, so they no longer start offset by the scene's position and jump into place, and anchored to their trigger's layout box, so a press that grows the glass around the trigger (a toolbar, a button) no longer moves the open menu.
- Glass costs less to draw: Clear glass skips its blur (it was too small to see) and draws its tone in one pass, a Lens with no tone draws none, and Regular panels reuse their frost for the softer copy instead of blurring twice. `aberration` turns on a Lens's colored fringes, which pressed switch and slider thumbs use; other lenses, such as a tab bar's selection, no longer pay for them.
- The rim's edge pixel no longer lets the page behind show through. The content under glass is now cut away only where the glass covers it fully, and the lit face is masked once, so a partly covered edge pixel blends exactly. Over a light page this showed as a bright line just inside the outline, which made the outline look heavier than macOS's.
- The outline fades out by 45 degrees around a corner, as on macOS, and dark Regular glass darkens bright backdrops more with its outline, controls a little and panels up to 71%. The rim light reaches a little further around corners.
- Glass that floats (menus, popovers, dialogs, and any surface with `floating`) looks like a panel at any size, so a two-item submenu and a long one look alike.
- A surface's look (material, appearance, tint, frost, refraction, `floating`, `aberration`) now changes in place, through the new `GlassSceneController.updateSurface`, instead of removing and adding the surface again. Switching a scene between Clear and Regular no longer redraws its glass from scratch or moves anything anchored to it, such as an open menu.
- A switch's thumb springs along its own track, never toward where it sits on screen: a switch in a menu no longer slides when the menu opens or moves into place, and its new side never shows for a frame before the spring starts. A new popup stays hidden for one more frame after its glass is drawn, while its rows settle into the shape the scene gives them.
- Tinted glass no longer breaks its scene: the tint and tone passes gave two filter results the same name, so the browser rejected the whole filter.
- `refraction` now defaults by size: half the shorter side up to 60px for Regular, 0.65 of it for Clear, a quarter for Lens.
- `floating` gives glass that floats above the window, such as menus, popovers and dialogs, the deeper shadow macOS casts for them. Popup surfaces and menus set it.
- Pressed thumbs lift as macOS's do. A switch's grows to about 1.55 times its size (a 32x20 thumb to 50x31) and a slider's to 1.25 times, over 210ms. The lens magnifies the track, passes its pixels unchanged, shows colored fringes where it bends, and casts a faint ring. While dragged it stretches up to 2.5% along its path and wobbles once when it stops, instead of stretching up to 18%. The switch track brightens while its thumb is held.
- Interactive glass responds to a press as macOS glass does: it grows 2.5px wider at any size, keeping its proportions, rather than by a share of its size; a 36px light gathers under the pointer and the face brightens a little; on release it settles back in about 200ms, dipping just under its size. A drag no longer stretches it.

- Glass looks like macOS's. Its materials follow the filter values Core Animation gives `NSGlassEffectView` in an active window on macOS 27, checked against captures over black, gray, white, text and a photo in both appearances, to within a few levels of brightness:
  - **Clear** keeps the backdrop sharp, lifts it (black reads as 12% gray, colors keep their saturation) and bends it at the rim, in either appearance.
  - **Regular** compresses bright backdrops in dark appearance and lifts dark ones in light, keeping their hue, and casts a faint shadow around itself.
  - **The rim** carries a key light along the top and bottom, white on its outer edge and fading over a few pixels, and a fine dark line just outside the sides, instead of an even outline.
  - Each surface frosts with one blur instead of two blurs blended together, which costs less.
- `frost` (0 to 1) moves Regular and Clear glass from see-through to frosted, like the Liquid Glass slider in macOS's Appearance settings. Set it on a scene, a surface or a control:

  ```tsx
  <GlassScene material="regular" frost={0.5}>
    …
  </GlassScene>
  ```

- `GlassSwitch` follows its own size. Set its `width` and `height` and the thumb fills the height inside the padding at a 32:20 ratio and travels the width that remains, in left-to-right and right-to-left text. You no longer need to style `.lg-switch-thumb` or `--lg-switch-travel`, which is gone. The default 54 × 24 switch looks the same as before:

  ```tsx
  <GlassSwitch aria-label="Wi-Fi" style={{ width: 44, height: 20 }} />
  ```

- Menus open and close at once: the glass no longer rebuilds its filter on every frame while a menu appears or leaves.
- The scene no longer prepares maps along animation paths, blends maps between shapes, or merges a popup's glass with its trigger's. Each surface draws the one map of its shape.

- A slider's fill and a switch's "on" track can be colored from CSS with `--lg-accent`, including a theme's CSS variable, when no `tint` is set. Before, the default gray was written on the element and no stylesheet could change it.

- Pressed and dragged glass stays where it is. It grows under a press and stretches toward a drag in place, instead of following the pointer off its spot, and it grows and stretches about a third less. Switch thumbs and the tab bar's lens still slide along their track.

### Breaking changes

- `GlassTabsIndicator` is now the resting plate, `material="regular"` by default instead of `"lens"`; the lens appears only under a press, in an element of its own in the scene's root. `GlassTabsList` defaults to `interactive={false}`. `attachSelectionLens` takes the lens through the returned `setLens(lens, root)` and an optional `onLifted` callback, and returns a `SelectionLens`.

- Materials changed look; see Improved. `material` now defaults to `"regular"`, as on macOS, instead of `"clear"`. A new `"lens"` material is the old unfrosted look, for magnifiers: pressed control thumbs, the tab bar's selection and the color picker use it. To keep glass that does not frost, use `material="lens"`.
- `@glass-sdk/liquid-glass/core` no longer exports `materials`. `glassRecipe(options)` returns the frost and tone a material uses, and `refraction` its default rim travel.

- Morphing menus and toolbars are removed. `GlassSurface` no longer accepts `morphFrom`, `morph`, `neck`, `morphEnter` or `fluid`, and `GlassMenuContent` no longer accepts `morph` or `neck`. Remove those props:

  ```diff
  - <GlassSurface morphFrom={triggerRef} morph="detach" fluid>
  + <GlassSurface>
  ```

  Toolbar groups created by a new `GlassToolbarSpacer` now appear in place instead of splitting off their neighbor.

- `@glass-sdk/liquid-glass/dom` no longer exports `attachGeometryMotion`, `radiusPixels`, `GeometryAnimator`, `GeometryMotionOptions`, `GlassMorph`, `LensFrame`, `LensPlacement` or `LensWaypoint`. `SurfaceOptions` drops `morphFrom`, `morph`, `fluid`, `neck` and `morphEnter`, and gains `popup` for a surface whose scene should rise above sibling scenes while it is present. `SurfaceAnimator.frame` no longer receives a `quiet` argument.
- `@glass-sdk/liquid-glass/core` no longer exports `smoothUnion`, `smoothMin`, `roundRectDistance` or `RoundRect`, and `springs.morph` is gone.
- `GlassMorph` is no longer exported from the package root.

### Fixed

- In Chromium, a glass surface holding other glass (a dialog with a glass close button, a toast with a glass action) keeps its glass while it fades or scrolls. Fading it used to cut its glass off from the page, which showed as a flat color while it moved, and scrolling it carried its glass away with its content.

- A newly mounted glass popup remains focusable while its outline is measured, so dialogs can focus their first field as they open.
