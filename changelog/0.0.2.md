title: Faster glass in every engine, and a smaller download
---

### New

- `GlassContent layout="scroll"` makes the content layer the scroll viewport. It keeps glass and blur still while Safari scrolls. Pass its ref to `GlassScrollEdges`:

  ```tsx
  const viewport = useRef<HTMLDivElement>(null);

  <GlassScene style={{ height: 320, overflow: "hidden" }}>
    <GlassContent
      layout="scroll"
      ref={viewport}
      tabIndex={0}
      role="region"
      aria-label="Reading list"
      style={{ scrollPaddingBlock: 80 }}
    >
      {children}
    </GlassContent>
    <GlassScrollEdges target={viewport} size={80} blur={20} />
  </GlassScene>;
  ```

  Before, you wrapped `GlassContent layout="flow"` in your own scrolling `div`. That still works.

### Improved

- An app that imports one component downloads about a third as much up front. The package ships one module per component, so bundlers drop the components and Base UI parts you don't import, and the GPU renderer loads on demand the first time glass needs it. Importing `GlassSurface` or `GlassButton` costs 46 to 47 KB gzipped, down from 139 KB.
- Firefox draws each surface's glass from a live copy of the content in its own layer, so unchanged glass isn't drawn again. Scrolling, menus, and switches use about a seventh of the CPU they did.
- `GlassProgressiveBlur` and `GlassScrollEdges` no longer need images or WebGPU, and now render in Firefox and Safari as well as Chrome.
- In Safari, a menu opens or closes with one redraw, where every frame of the animation used to take about 200 ms. Pressed glass keeps its outline instead of stretching, dragging glass runs at 60 fps, and glass follows page zoom and pinch.
- In Chrome, a surface that moves as a whole gets its own layer while it moves, so dragging glass costs about 40% less GPU time.
- `GlassContent` keeps your props, ref, and children on its own element inside stable wrappers. Target its class rather than relying on it being a direct child of the scene.

### Fixed

- In Safari, displacement now covers every sampled pixel, and filters survive partial repaints.
